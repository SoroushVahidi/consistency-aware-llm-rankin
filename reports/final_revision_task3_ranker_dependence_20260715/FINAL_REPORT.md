## 2. Score coverage and abstention findings

Computed exhaustively per dataset, per ranker, per candidate-pool size (canonical: SciDocs/FiQA/BRIGHT P=20, HotpotQA P=10; Task 1 larger pool: SciDocs/FiQA/BRIGHT P=50, HotpotQA P=35), tracing every candidate pair through: eligibility -> native-tie abstention -> missing-score abstention -> vote-margin-threshold abstention -> retained vote -> (per regime) support qualification -> aggregate-threshold qualification -> final graph edge, without double-counting opposite directions.

Tables: `tables/coverage_per_query.csv`, `tables/coverage_aggregate.csv`, `tables/pair_funnel_per_query.csv`, `tables/pair_funnel_aggregate.csv`.

Key findings (canonical pool):

- BM25/TF-IDF candidate-pool coverage ranges `74.8%`-`99.6%` across datasets; BRIGHT is the low end for every ranker.
- MiniLM coverage is consistently and substantially lower: `44.3%` (FiQA) to `71.2%` (HotpotQA), versus `75`-`100%` for the two lexical rankers. This is a representational limitation (MiniLM's own top-ranked documents overlap the RRF-fused pool less), not evidence that MiniLM "disagrees" less.
- Native-score ties are rare (under ~1.5% of eligible pairs) except on HotpotQA, where all three rankers tie on `1.3%`-`3.7%` of eligible pairs.
- Final retained-edge fraction (of all possible pairs) varies sharply by regime: e.g. SciDocs canonical `ms2` retains `36.1%` of pairs as edges vs. `95.7%` under `ms1`.

## 3. Pairwise ranker correlations

Table: `tables/rank_correlation_summary.csv` (macro-averaged Kendall's tau-b and Spearman's rho over documents scored by both rankers, per-query distribution in `tables/rank_correlation_per_query.csv`).

| Dataset | tau_b(BM25,TFIDF) | tau_b(BM25,MiniLM) | tau_b(TFIDF,MiniLM) |
|---|---|---|---|
| SciDocs | 0.452 | 0.214 | 0.175 |
| FiQA | 0.312 | 0.175 | 0.187 |
| HotpotQA | 0.562 | 0.436 | 0.434 |
| BRIGHT | 0.142 | 0.182 | 0.171 |

BM25-TF-IDF is the most correlated pair on 3 of 4 datasets, but the correlation is moderate everywhere (never reaches 0.6) and BRIGHT is a genuine exception where BM25-MiniLM is marginally higher. This directly answers Task 3 concern #1: BM25 and TF-IDF are correlated lexical signals in most, but not all, benchmarks, and never strongly enough to treat as redundant.

## 4. Top-k overlap and directional agreement

Tables: `tables/topk_overlap_per_query.csv`, `tables/topk_overlap_summary.csv` (Jaccard, overlap coefficient, RBO at p=0.9, depths 5/10/20/35-or-50, computed on each ranker's own full stored score list, not the fused candidate pool), `tables/directional_agreement_margin_correlation.csv` (directional agreement + raw/normalized margin correlation on jointly-scored candidate-pool pairs).

- Directional agreement (canonical pool) for BM25-TF-IDF: `72.0%` (SciDocs), `62.7%` (FiQA), `81.7%` (HotpotQA), but only `57.3%` on BRIGHT -- the *lowest* of the three pairs there (BM25-MiniLM is `60.4%`).
- Raw-space margin correlations are noted as scale-sensitive (BM25 unbounded vs. TF-IDF/MiniLM roughly `[0,1]`/`[-1,1]`-bounded); normalized-space correlations are reported as the scale-controlled comparison in the same table.
- RBO was implemented per Webber, Moffat & Zobel (2010)'s extrapolated formula; an initial implementation bug (missing the `(1-p)/p` scaling and the tail-extrapolation term) was caught by a unit test (`test_rbo_identical_lists_is_one`) before being used in any reported number, fixed, and all coverage/dependence tables were regenerated from the corrected code.

## 5. Mutual-pair vote-configuration attribution

Table: `tables/mutual_pair_attribution_summary.csv` (raw per-pair rows in `tables/mutual_pair_attribution_raw.csv`).

| Dataset (canonical) | Lexical-pair-vs-MiniLM % | Single-vs-single % |
|---|---|---|
| SciDocs | 14.8 | 78.9 |
| FiQA | 5.9 | 89.8 |
| HotpotQA | 26.8 | 66.5 |
| BRIGHT | 3.8 | 91.1 |

The reviewer-hypothesized "BM25+TF-IDF vote together against MiniLM" configuration is a minority everywhere (`3.8%`-`26.8%`); single-voter-vs-single-voter disagreement between an arbitrary pair of rankers dominates (`66.5%`-`91.1%`). **The hypothesis is empirically rejected as the dominant mechanism.** This holds at both the canonical and Task 1 larger pool (see the full summary table for the `task1_larger` rows).

## 6. Leave-one-ranker-out structural results

Tables: `tables/leave_one_out_structural_summary.csv`, full per-query records in `outputs/leave_one_out_per_query_records.json`.

Four variants share the same (canonical RRF-fused) candidate pool: `all_three` (regimes `ms2`/`ms1`/`ms1_drop_mutual`), `pair_bm25_tfidf`, `pair_bm25_minilm`, `pair_tfidf_minilm` (each with two-ranker regimes `pair_unanimous`/`pair_any`/`pair_any_drop_mutual`, named explicitly rather than reusing `ms1`/`ms2` since support=2 means unanimity with 2 voters but majority with 3).

- No two-ranker variant eliminates cyclicity or shows a consistent redundancy pattern.
- `pair_bm25_tfidf` has *more* mean mutual pairs than the other two pairs on FiQA (`41.1` vs `11.9`/`12.6`), SciDocs, and BRIGHT, but *fewer* on HotpotQA (`3.4` vs `5.3`/`6.0`) -- the pattern is dataset-dependent, not a uniform "lexical redundancy" story.
- Structural metrics (triangle count, SCC size, repair-active fraction, removed-edge weight) are reported per variant/regime/dataset/pool in the summary table.

## 8. Pre-pool versus post-pool normalization results

Tables: `tables/pre_post_normalization_structural_summary.csv`, `tables/pre_post_normalization_removed_edge_overlap.csv`, `tables/pre_post_normalization_retrieval_summary.csv`, `tables/pre_post_normalization_retrieval_statistics.csv`, `tables/pre_post_normalization_active_family_holm.csv`.

Threshold policy (declared before inspecting results, per `manifests/pre_post_normalization_run_summary.json`): both constructions use an independently-defined per-construction median (q=0.5) of that construction's own pooled pairwise-margin distribution, so neither construction's threshold is derived from the other's retention target.

- Structural statistics are nearly identical between `post_pool_minmax` and `pre_pool_minmax` (e.g. SciDocs `ms1` canonical: `153.9` vs. `154.1` mean edges).
- Removed-edge Jaccard overlap between the two constructions, averaged per dataset/pool/regime cell, is `0.748`-`1.00` across all 24 cells (individual per-query values can be `0.0` when only one construction needed to repair that particular query -- this is expected and does not contradict the cell-level agreement; both figures are reported).
- Pre-specified active family (`ms1` regime only, both constructions, 2 pair methods x 4 datasets x 2 pools = **32 cells**): **0 significant** after Holm.
- **Answer to the Task 3 question**: candidate-pool size still changes graph structure substantially (per Task 1), but that change is attributable to the change in candidate-set composition itself, not to an interaction between pool restriction and normalization order -- pre/post-pool normalization order is not a material confound at the tested pool sizes.

## 9. `ms2` density/sparsity explanation

Tables: `tables/ms2_sparsity_per_query.csv`, `tables/ms2_sparsity_summary.csv`.

- Across all 684 dataset x query x pool cells evaluated under `ms2` (342 usable queries x 2 pool sizes across 4 datasets), mean density ranges `0.013`-`0.27` and mean edge counts range `21`-`191` -- non-trivial, not near-empty.
- Two-cycles (mutual pairs) are **combinatorially impossible** under `ms2`: support >= 2-of-3 for a direction means both directions of a pair would require 4 total ranker votes from only 3 rankers. Verified structurally and covered by a unit test (`test_ms2_mutual_pairs_structurally_impossible`).
- Longer (3+-node) cycles remain theoretically possible under majority consensus but were **never observed**: the largest SCC across all 684 cells was always exactly 1 node.
- **Conclusion**: `ms2`'s empirical acyclicity is a combination of a provable combinatorial fact (no 2-cycles) and an observed, but not theoretically guaranteed, near-transitivity of majority consensus for longer cycles -- not simply "the graphs are too sparse to cycle."

## 11. Raw score-distribution summaries

Table: `tables/raw_score_distribution_summary.csv` (min/max/mean/median/std/IQR, mean per-query range, mean/median pairwise-margin, canonical candidate-pool scores actually used by the pipeline).

- BM25 is unbounded and wide-range: e.g. BRIGHT `25.7`-`652.6`; HotpotQA `11.8`-`98.3`.

- This scale disparity is the quantitative basis for treating raw cross-ranker correlations as scale-sensitive diagnostics (Section 4) and supports the existing manuscript finding that raw-margin vote construction is BM25-scale-dominated.

## 12. Final data-quality conflict taxonomy

Full taxonomy: `TAXONOMY.md`. Four operational categories, each with observable diagnostic / failure mode / mitigation:

- **A. Construction-induced artifacts** -- incompatible scales, missing-score handling, manufactured ties, unreported thresholds, candidate-dependent normalization. All measured directly in this task rather than assumed.
- **B. Genuine evidence conflict** -- moderate-not-extreme ranker correlation, mutual pairs dominated by single-vs-single disagreement (not a lexical bloc), residual cyclicity after artifact controls that varies by dataset.
- **C. Coverage/representation limitations** -- MiniLM's lower pool coverage, small-pool understatement (addressed by testing both pool sizes throughout), the BRIGHT exception to the BM25-TF-IDF correlation pattern.
- **D. Repair-induced changes** -- structural consistency improvement vs. ranking-metric changes vs. retrieval-metric changes, each measured and reported separately (repair-active fraction vs. helped/harmed/unchanged counts vs. Holm significance).

Summary judgment integrated into `main.tex`'s Discussion/Limitations.

## 17. Remaining limitations

- The dependence and mutual-pair-attribution analysis uses the same stored score files and RRF-fused candidate pools as the manuscript's primary protocol; it does not re-derive upstream retrieval with different rankers or parameterizations.
- Leave-one-ranker-out and pre/post-pool normalization use a fixed numeric or independently-quantile-derived vote threshold rather than a full retention-matched search per subset/construction, by explicit design (to isolate the variable of interest); this means their edge counts are not directly comparable in scale to the manuscript's primary retention-matched protocol.
- RBO, top-k overlap, and rank correlation are computed on each ranker's own full stored score list (depths 50/50/35/50 per Task 1's verified common complete depths); deeper native rankings are not available.
- The `ms2` longer-cycle acyclicity finding is empirical (0/684 observed cells), not a proof; a future dataset or protocol could in principle produce a majority-consensus cycle.
- The mutual-pair attribution taxonomy (lexical-pair-vs-MiniLM / single-vs-single / etc.) is defined for `ms1`'s min_support=1 regime; its exact percentages would differ under a different regime definition, though the qualitative conclusion (single-vs-single dominates) was checked at both pool sizes.
